## Review Body

I reviewed the paper as a statistician working on Bayesian linear models and resampling. I checked every proof in Appendix A line by line, recomputed the five-encoder means in Table 2 from the per-encoder values in Table 4, and cross-checked the Section 4 prose against Tables 1, 3 and 5. The theory is clean and, as far as I can verify, correct. The empirical section is carefully designed, but it is under-powered statistically in exactly the places the abstract leans on, and it has text-table inconsistencies that need a provenance audit before the numbers can be trusted.

Proof audit summary. Theorem 2 follows from Isserlis: contracting the fourth moment with symmetric P and Q gives Cov = 2 tr(P Sigma_AB Q Sigma_BA) and Var = 2 tr((P Sigma_A)^2). The sample statement, a Frobenius cosine of ridge smoothers, is the plug-in version of the same expression. Proposition 3(i) holds after multiplying through by rho squared. Proposition 3(ii) holds, but the ratio is sum r_j^2 over sqrt(d_A d_B), which equals the mean squared canonical correlation only when d_A = d_B (Contribution 1 states it without that qualification). In Corollary 4, the bounds k/(k + m/4) at most epsilon for m at least 4k/epsilon, and 1 - 4m/k for k at least 4m/epsilon, both check, as do the CKA side-conditions. In Proposition 5, the substitution giving prior strength rho/c^2 is right, and the Cauchy-Schwarz equality condition (flat nonzero spectrum, c not equal to 1) is right. In Proposition 6, 1 - CKA^2 = eta(s^2-1)^2/((1+eta)(1+s^4 eta)) is exact, and CKA at least CKA^2 gives the stated bound. Lemma 7 is the standard trace identity. Lemma 9's sampling covariance and the w^4 weighting (eigenvalues of M Sigma are w^2) check. In Proposition 10, both resolvent-difference factorisations are valid and each resolvent norm is at most s^-2. I found no mathematical error. The issues below are about scope and use, not correctness.

Recomputation summary. The Table 2 means and min-max ranges agree with Table 4 within two-decimal rounding for all 18 cells I checked (EU width raw and partial, AU raw). Examples: the 10% partial cell is (0.40+0.21+0.25+0.28+0.17)/5 = 0.262, reported 0.26. The re-test floor is (0.61+0.52+0.55+0.55+0.58)/5 = 0.562, reported 0.56. The 46% EU retention is 0.26/0.56 = 0.464. The AU fraction-of-ceiling values (0.48 to 0.61 against a 0.70 ceiling) are consistent within rounding. None of these values falls under a bounded recompute procedure (see Arithmetic Receipts).

### W1: Section 4.3 prose contradicts Table 3 on rank and ratio values
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, scale-transform paragraph "bootstrap width between 0.38 and 1.84" ; "Laplace MI rank agreement down to 0.72"
**Confidence**: 5 (direct comparison of printed numbers)

For c in [0.1, 10], Table 3 gives a width mean ratio of 0.56 to 1.52, but the text says 0.38 to 1.84. Table 3 gives a minimum Laplace rank agreement of 0.85 (c = 10), but the text says 0.72. It gives a minimum width rank agreement of 0.94 (c = 0.1), but the text says 0.89. The tail paragraph has the same problem. At s = 4, Table 3 gives a Laplace ratio of 1.8, a width ratio of 1.30 and mutual k-NN 0.68, while the text says up to 2.3, up to 1.39, and drops to 0.62. A stray 4.3 also appears in that table row. The qualitative conclusion (EU moves at fixed weight decay while alignment is invariant) holds under either set of numbers. However, the P5 and L9 pre-registered outcomes in Table 1 are read off these values, and the AI-use statement says every number is generated by script from logged results. Two inconsistent sets of numbers suggest the text and the table come from different runs or configurations. The authors should regenerate both from one logged run and state which run Table 1 uses.

### W2: The headline Sρ-versus-CKA comparison has no inferential support and uses non-independent pairs
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4, first paragraph "EU agreement at Spearman 0.83, against 0.64 for CKA"
**Confidence**: 4 (standard correlation-comparison inference; exact dependence structure unknown to me)

The abstract and the falsifier S rest on a difference between two Spearman correlations computed over the same 30 encoder pairs. Those 30 pairs are not independent: they are 10 encoder pairs at 3 depths, and each encoder appears in 4 pairs. As a rough illustration that treats the 30 pairs as i.i.d. (which flatters the precision), the Fisher-z 95% intervals are about [0.67, 0.92] for 0.83 and [0.36, 0.81] for 0.64, and they overlap. An independent-samples z for the difference is about 1.6. The correct test is a dependent-correlation comparison (Steiger, or a bootstrap over encoders or encoder pairs that respects the crossed design), and it would also need the Sρ-CKA correlation across pairs. None is reported. Falsifier S (Sρ no better than CKA) therefore cannot be judged on point estimates. Required: a cluster bootstrap over encoders (or a permutation scheme) giving a CI for the difference in Spearman correlations, both at the headline column and across all Table 5 columns.

### W3: The partial-agreement noise floor is a Monte Carlo artefact of M = 50, and the comparisons are not reliability-adjusted
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: table: Table 2, row re-test floor, column EU partial (0.56)
**Confidence**: 4 (psychometric reliability reasoning applied to ensemble resampling)

Two ensembles with identical configuration agree at 1.00 raw but only 0.56 partial. After removing confidence and human entropy, about 44% of the residual rank variance of the EU score is therefore bootstrap Monte Carlo noise. That is plausible for a 5-95% quantile width estimated from 50 members, where each quantile rests on two or three extreme members. The M-stability study in Appendix B reports only raw Spearman (0.998 at M = 50), so the reliability of the metric the paper actually uses as its headline is uncharacterised as a function of M. This has two consequences. (a) The floor is a property of the estimator budget, not of the models, so the 0.26 versus 0.56 contrast would move with M. (b) Under classical attenuation, the reliability-corrected agreement is about 0.26/0.56 = 0.46 for 10% vs 100%, and about 0.36/0.56 = 0.64 for disjoint halves. If the 10% head is itself less reliable, the corrected value is higher still. The paper disattenuates every AU number against the CIFAR-10H ceiling but does not do the same for EU. The qualitative conclusion (data matter) probably survives, but the abstract's 0.26 against 0.56 overstates the magnitude. In addition, only one 10% subset and one pair of halves are drawn per encoder, so variability over subset draws is unknown. The checklist answer that re-test floors quantify seed variability does not cover this. Required: partial-agreement reliability versus M (or a larger M), reliability-corrected agreements, and at least a few subset redraws with intervals.

### W4: Pre-registered falsifier E2a was unreachable, and the S and E2b criteria lack operational thresholds
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: table: Table 1, row E2a (partial EU agreement 10% vs 100% above 0.9 falsifies)
**Confidence**: 4 (follows from the paper's own re-test floor)

Agreement between two different configurations cannot be expected to exceed the re-test reliability of a single configuration, and that reliability is 0.56 for partial EU (Table 2). A threshold of 0.9 could therefore never have been crossed. Not falsified carries no evidential weight, yet the abstract presents the pre-registered predictions as a strength. Likewise, E2b (AU degrades as much as EU) and S (Sρ no better than CKA) specify no tolerance or test, so their verdicts are judgement calls on point estimates. The Spec row adds an outcome category, partly, that the falsifier framework does not define. The paper should restate E2a's informativeness honestly, give the operational decision rules that were frozen for E2b and S if any existed, and otherwise relabel those verdicts as post hoc readings.

### W5: The prior-strength dependence of Sρ is not tested empirically, because all heads sit in the small-rho regime
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: table: Table 5, rows Sρ→0, Sρ (head's ρ) and linear predictivity, column width partial
**Confidence**: 4 (direct reading of Table 5 plus the deff/d above 0.98 statement in §4.4)

The theoretical novelty is that EU agreement depends on spectral overlap weighted by w_i(rho)^2 at the head's own prior strength. Section 4.4, however, reports deff(rho)/d above 0.98 for every head, which puts Sρ at its canonical-correlation limit. Table 5 confirms this: Sρ→0 and Sρ at the head's rho agree to within 0.01 in every column (0.83 vs 0.83 on the headline column). Linear predictivity also reaches 0.83 on that column. The cross-encoder evidence therefore shows only that CCA-type indices order partial EU agreement better than CKA. It does not show that matching rho matters, and it does not single out Sρ among the non-CKA baselines. Mutual k-NN is within 0.03 of Sρ on raw width, MI and BLR. Recommendation (i), Sρ at the head's own rho, is accordingly stronger than the data license. The same regime makes closed-form EU nearly a leverage score, which the paper itself notes is invariant to invertible maps, so the real-data tests of Propositions 5 and 6 rest entirely on the softmax estimators, which the theory does not cover. Required: either a sweep of weight decay that moves deff/d well below 1, showing that Sρ at the matched rho beats both Sρ→0 and the CCA limit, or a narrowing of the claim to CCA-type indices.

### W6: The claim that CKA is the weakest index is contradicted by Table 5
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4, first paragraph "the weakest of the indices we tested"
**Confidence**: 5 (direct table reading)

Linear predictivity scores below CKA in four of the five Table 5 columns: 0.41 vs 0.66 (width), 0.44 vs 0.67 (MI), 0.56 vs 0.76 (BLR) and 0.53 vs 0.68 (AU). It ties Sρ only on the headline partial column. The sentence should be corrected, and the main text should report the full Table 5 pattern rather than only the column that favours Sρ.

### W7: Lemma 9 is applied outside its assumptions, and the L9 count does not match the design
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, final sentence "Laplace EU under tail reshaping; this held in 6 of 6"
**Confidence**: 4 (bootstrap theory for regularised estimators)

Lemma 9 covers fixed-design linear ridge with residual resampling. The experiments use a Poisson (case-resampling, random-design) bootstrap of softmax heads. For a regularised estimator, a case-resampling covariance depends on the labels, through the residuals and the shrinkage bias, so it is not a shrunk posterior. Confirming L9 is therefore not evidence for the lemma's mechanism. It also undercuts the Discussion's appeal to labels not entering the posterior variance as an EU-specific mechanism, because the headline estimator is label-dependent. Separately, Section 4.3 lists three tail settings (s in {0.5, 2, 4}) but reports 6 of 6. If the count is 3 settings times 2 metrics, then at s = 0.5 the rank agreements tie at 1.00 versus 1.00, which is not moving less. State what the six units are and how ties were scored.

### W8: The data-fraction pairs do not instantiate the mechanism of Corollary 8 under the stated objective
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: equation: Appendix B, Heads objective (weighted-mean cross-entropy plus wd/2 times squared norm of W)
**Confidence**: 3 (depends on whether weight decay was re-tuned for subset heads, which the paper does not state)

Corollary 8 illustrates rho1 not equal to rho2 through the same lambda with different n, and Section 4.1 uses a fixed ridge in that sense. The real heads, however, minimise a mean loss plus wd/2 times the squared norm of W, so rho = wd is independent of n at fixed weight decay. The 10% vs 100% and disjoint-halves pairs therefore have the same rho, and their disagreement comes from covariance estimation error and softmax curvature, not from a prior change. Only the wd divided by 10 and wd times 10 pairs instantiate Corollary 8. The paper also does not say whether weight decay was re-selected for the subset heads. Please clarify this, and attribute the E2 data-fraction results to the data term rather than to Corollary 8.

### W11: Proposition 10 does not justify evaluating Sρ on training plus evaluation inputs
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3, after Proposition 10 "points the ratio reached ≈5 (Appendix A). This is why"
**Confidence**: 4 (logical reading of the statement)

Proposition 10 bounds operator-norm differences between unnormalised posterior covariances on observed points. Sρ is a scale-normalised Frobenius cosine, and nothing in Proposition 10 bounds it, on any input set. The inference to evaluate Sρ on training plus evaluation inputs is therefore a design choice that needs its own justification, for example an ablation comparing training-only and training-plus-test Sρ.
